Integrations-Cockpit

From Excel exports to “who do I call this week, and why?”

Challenge by Stadt Bern, Asylsozialdienst · Team IntegrationCockpit

Hi, we are team IntegrationCockpit. We took the challenge of the Asylsozialdienst of the City of Bern. In one sentence: we turn the Excel exports they already have into a cockpit that tells every social worker who to call this week, and why – and tells management where the service stands against its integration goals.

The problem

The ASD is paid only when refugees reach a goal – on time

Goal 1 · Language

A1 certificate within 3 years of entry

VA and FL, 16+

Goal 2 · Work

6 months of work ≥ 60 %, or education Sek II / tertiary

VA and FL, 16+, before end of responsibility

Goal 3 · Income

12 months without social assistance

The whole household, before end of responsibility

Today nobody sees systematically who is on track – or why some make it. The case management system can’t be changed; it can only export Excel.

The ASD’s funding is success-based. It is paid only when a client reaches one of three goals before a deadline: an A1 language certificate within three years, six months of work at 60 percent or more – or an apprenticeship – and twelve months without social assistance for the whole household. Today nobody can see systematically who is on track, who is about to miss a deadline, or why some clients make it and others don’t. And the case management system can’t be changed – it can only export Excel lists.

Our approach

Excel in, Excel out – next month, the same clicks

11 Excel exports

Read and check

Each file recognised by its columns, not its name. A data-quality report lists what is missing or wrong.

Apply the rules

Deadlines, status, the three goals. Every assumption sits in one readable file.

Results

Cockpit, weekly call list, patterns – and one Excel file with every list for daily work.

No integration with the case management system. It runs on one ASD computer.

Our principle is: Excel in, Excel out. The social workers drop in the exports exactly as the system produces them – German column names, Swiss dates. The tool recognises each file by its columns, not by its file name, and writes a data-quality report. Then it applies the rules for deadlines and the three goals. Every assumption we had to make is in one readable file, so the ASD can change it without a programmer. And the result comes back as Excel, so it fits their daily work. Next month: same files, same clicks.

Level one is what the ASD said it needs most: target versus actual. For every current client the tool works out whether the goal applies, the exact deadline, and whether it is reached. In our demo data that is 314 clients for the language goal. 48 percent of those whose deadline has come made it on time; 16 are at risk because the deadline is less than six months away; 70 should have had A1 by now. The same exists for work and for income. And it is broken down per social worker and per year of entry. Important: these are synthetic people – we generated them in the exact format of the ASD exports, because we did not get real data.

Level four is the cockpit: one row per client, a traffic light for each goal. Green reached, yellow on track, red at risk, grey not applicable. A social worker opens the tool on Monday and gets a call list: red cases first, then the nearest deadline, each with the reason in plain words. The tool is also fair to clients: someone who is three months into a full-time job that will reach six months before the deadline stays yellow, not red. Management gets the same numbers as totals per social worker and per year of entry.

Level two asks why. Among everyone whose deadline has passed, the tool compares groups. People literate in Latin script reach A1 on time in 62 percent of cases, the others in 35. Schooling, motivation and psychological burden matter too – and those last two come from the free-text Situationsanalyse, read by AI. Two rules we hold to: groups smaller than ten people are never shown, and there is never a score for an individual. And we label these as associations, not causes – for example, people in literacy courses do worse, simply because those courses go to the people who need them most.

AI, used carefully

A local model reads the free-text Situationsanalyse

Word or PDF report

Written per household at entry

Names removed

Also phone, address and numbers

Local model

Runs on the ASD computer; nothing is sent out

5 features

Burden, motivation, pace, childcare, network

Human review

The social worker checks and corrects

90 % correct on average (86–94 % per feature), measured on 112 demo people whose truth we know

When a report says nothing, the model tends to guess – so a person always reviews before results are used

Output is an Excel table – the AI never decides anything about a client

The Situationsanalyse is free text, so we use AI – carefully. Before the model sees a report, names, phone numbers, addresses and ID numbers are removed. The model runs locally with Ollama on the ASD’s own computer, so nothing leaves the network. It turns each report into five features: psychological burden, motivation, learning pace, childcare as an obstacle, and social network. On our demo data it is right 90 percent of the time. But we saw that when a report says nothing, it tends to guess. That is why a social worker reviews the table before it is used – the AI never decides anything about a client.

Data protection by design · revDSG

Nothing leaves the ASD network

QuestionOur answer
Where is data processed?On one ASD computer, in memory only. Nothing is stored, usage statistics are off, the app answers only on that computer.
Who sees what?Social workers: their own cases. Management: totals. Patterns: groups of 10 or more, never a score per person.
What leaves the network?Nothing. The AI model runs locally, and names and numbers are removed before it reads a report.
Can we trust the result?Every rule is in one readable file; every Excel export carries an “Assumptions” and a “Data quality” sheet; 54 automated tests.
The data here is about asylum, social assistance and health, so data protection was a design rule from the start, not an add-on. Everything runs on one ASD computer, in memory; nothing is stored and nothing is sent out – including the AI. Social workers see only their own cases, management sees totals, and the pattern analysis only ever shows groups of ten or more. And every result is explainable: the rules are in one readable file, and every Excel file says which assumptions it used and what was wrong in the data.

Impact

How it scores on the four lenses

Desirable

Social workers get a weekly call list with reasons; management finally sees target vs actual – from exports they already have.

Viable

Funding is success-based: every goal caught before its deadline, and every reached goal not yet claimed, is money for the service.

Feasible

Works today on the export format, with no integration. Runs on one computer; real exports only need a column check.

Ethical

No individual scoring, groups of 10+, local AI with human review, every assumption visible and changeable.

On the jury’s four lenses. Desirable: it answers the question social workers actually ask every Monday, and gives management target versus actual, using files they already have. Viable: the service is paid per goal reached, so every case caught before its deadline, and every goal reached but not yet claimed, is money. Feasible: it works today on the export format, needs no integration, and runs on one computer. Ethical: no individual scores, only groups of ten or more, AI that runs locally with a human in the loop, and every assumption visible.
